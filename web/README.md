# Earthrise — web app

Next.js 16 app for the CLPS Lunar Mission Browser (NASA Space Apps 2026, team Astroy/Ed).

## Setup

Node.js 22 (20.9 to 26 works; `.nvmrc` holds 22 for nvm and fnm), and npm run here, in `web/`. The root README's "Run it on your computer" covers installing Node and uv on Windows, macOS and Linux, filling `public/data`, the environment file, another port and what to do when npm fails.

    npm ci
    npm run dev

Between the two, fill `public/data` from `pipeline/`: `uv run python -m lunar_pipeline.cli sync-local` copies the committed data, enough for every page; the root README's step 3 builds the tiles and the terrain before `sync-local --tiles` copies them. Before pushing: `npx vitest run` (`--maxWorkers=2` on a small computer), `npm run lint`, `npx tsc --noEmit`, `npm run build`, and `npm run validation:tests` for the badge (root README, "Before you push").

`.npmrc` sets `legacy-peer-deps=true` because npm 10's peer resolver crashes on vitest 5's peer set. `package.json`'s `engines` accepts Node 20.9 to 26, the versions a clean install, the tests and the build were checked on.

## Engine

`src/lib/engine/` is a pure-TypeScript port of the Python pipeline science (no DOM, no React):
time (UTC ↔ TDB), geometry (ENU az/el with parallax, south polar stereographic), ephemeris
(Catmull-Rom over the hourly MOON_ME table), tiles (zlib int16 DEM tiles, bilinear sampler),
horizon (nested-grid skyline with fallback bands), metrics (sunlit / Earth-visible series,
prefix-sum window index, best-window search).

Parity gates (all in `npm test`): JPL Horizons az/el < 0.002° (achieved 2.7e-5°), Python masks
< 0.01° (achieved 0.0005°, the JSON rounding quantum), metrics fractions < 1e-6 (exact).
Demo: `npm run engine:demo -- --site shackleton_center_rim_high` (mask in about 2 s cold, under 1.5 s warm in Node; `computeSiteMask` also reports DEM coverage and any tiles that failed to load).

## Routes

Every page keeps its first screen short (spec 20.1): explanations sit one tap away behind a button that says what it opens ("Why this happens", "How to read the chart", "About this view", "How these figures are counted", "How to read this", "Showing it to a class?"; `src/components/Fold.tsx`), and a test per page caps the words that show before a tap at 1440 and 390 px (`src/test/visible-words.ts`).

- `/`: home page (spec 23). Opens with the descent (see "The descent" below), whose first screen says what the product does, with "Fly to the south pole" and "See why the pole is hard" (to `#why`); then five numbered sections: 01, one real month at de Gerlache Rim 2 read in three steps (the Sun, Earth, the lander), hour by hour under the pointer or the arrow keys, on the black stage; 02 to 04 on paper in both themes: why the south pole is hard in three drawings of that month (the Sun's path, the two skylines 12 km apart, Earth's loop), a computed shadow picture of the ridge beside three real places (the ridge, and the Athena and Blue Ghost landers of March 2025, which open Explore at the first whole hour after each landed), and the checks against NASA, with a past mission as context; 05, the ways on: compare sites, ask in plain words, print the brief, run it offline (`/learn#offline`).
- `/sites`: "Choose a landing site". Every catalogue site in plain groups (the south pole's, the landers', the Apollo landings, other countries' landers and the coming CLPS landings, with the far side under a heading of its own), each saying what kind of point it is, with its best landing day in the chosen period, its time with power from that day, and its sunlight and contact with Earth over the first N days after it (`loadOverview` in the store works them out one site at a time; places that never see Earth sit under their own heading), a relief map of the pole with one pin per site (names written where they fit, `src/lib/viz/maplabels.ts`), and a form that computes any other place south of 60° S. A row opens Explore on the landing date it names, where every figure of the row is one Explore shows.
- `/explore`: the answer for one site and period. The answer says how long the power lasts and why, and offers the landing date inside the period that gives the longest time with power ("Land on 20 January instead"); a link searches the next 12 months. Below it: the numbers, a chart of the Sun, Earth and the battery hour by hour on one time axis, the ground (one sentence for the time bar's hour: is the site lit, can Earth be reached; then three tabs: "Shadow map" with north up, "3D from above" (the same light on the terrain) and "Horizon chart" (the hills all round with the Sun's and Earth's paths); a site with no polar terrain opens on the horizon chart and shows the globe in 3D), where the site is with its nearest neighbours within 300 km, and the assistant; the time bar stays at the bottom of the window. `?site=&start=&days=` opens a site and period.
- `/explore/stand`: "Stand on the Moon", the full-window view from the site (`src/components/stand/`), opened by "Stand at the site" beside the ground panel's tabs and under the answer. `?site=&start=&days=&hour=` as Explore plus the hour; `?bright=1` for "Brighter picture"; `?quality=fast` for weaker graphics (pixel ratio 0.75, no drawn detail, coarser shadow steps). It reuses the store, the words (`src/lib/viz/stand.ts`: the status line on the lander's power and radio, the headline, the key moments and their captions, the Sun and Earth labels, the line on what is measured and what is drawn, and the reasons under "Why this happens") and the time bar, which carries the key moments as dots, with Next to step to them and a speed for Play. Over the view the answer comes first: the headline on the status plate (its short form below 768 px), the next step under it, and from 768 px the lander's power and radio at the plate's foot with links to Earthrise, How it works and Accuracy; "More" opens today's card (on a phone the lander's state first, then the headline, what to watch, the better landing day, the reasons, "Brighter picture" for projectors and the links). On arrival one line over the view says "Press Play to watch the month go by · drag to look around" until the first turn, Play or Next; today's card shows what to watch and how to drag once it has gone. A small Easter egg stands opposite the lander, out of the opening view (`standResident` in `src/lib/viz/standOpening.ts`); it shares a boulder's geometry and material, so it adds no shader program, and it is off in demo mode and with `?bright=1`. The row of buttons ("Turn to: Sun, Earth, Lander", "Look up", "Show paths", "Hide the words") sits at the top right from 1280 px wide and above the time bar below that. Only the Sun, Earth and the lander are named until "Show paths", which shows the period's Sun and Earth paths (marked with the days of sunset, sunrise and Earth rising and setting), the level horizon where it clears the hills, the star names at night and your shadow's length. "Hide the words" leaves a bare time bar. Without WebGL 2 it shows the horizon chart. The development laptop's integrated graphics are not a target; the demo runs on a laptop with a discrete GPU. From 768 px wide the status plate carries the next step under its line (parts D2 and D3): the better landing day with its power and radio as the one gold action (else Compare sites), then one "Change ▾" holding Change site, Change date, Change lander (the forecast page's lander dialog, in place) and Compare sites (part F); on a phone the better day is a gold chip under the plate and the rest waits in today's card under More. The caption over the view (why a key moment happens, what looking up shows) docks on the compass plate from 768 px, which names its dots (Sun, Earth, lander); the time bar's flags are laid out by their measured widths in up to three rows.
- `/compare`: "Compare sites". Up to four sites in one table for the same period and lander: a small Sun and Earth strip per site (`src/components/compare/MiniStrip.tsx`), sunlight, Earth in view, longest night, time with power from the period's first day and the verdict, with one sentence above the table that names the best site for each measure, every tied site included (`src/lib/viz/compare.ts`). Empty, it offers two ready pairs; with two sites or more, a link searches the next 12 months for the landing day that is best on average.
- `/validate`: "Accuracy". It opens with "Three of four checks meet their criteria; past missions are context, not a check.", counted from the panels' own pass marks. Four checks, each with its pass mark, headline figure, plain sentence and what it shows, passes on, was tested on and leaves out (`src/lib/viz/checks.ts`, `src/lib/viz/pictures.ts`), and its table behind "Show the numbers": directions to the Sun and Earth against JPL Horizons (the live re-check runs through `/api/horizons`), sunlight and Earth in view against the LOLA maps worked out the maps' way, the app's shadows against 20 pictures from LRO's cameras (`data/validation/images.json`; this check misses its own mark, as 17 of the 20 pictures meet it), the power sums worked out in the browser, and, as context, predicted sunsets beside the reported ends of three missions. Then how sure the times are (`src/components/UncertaintySection.tsx`, from `data/uncertainty`), then the terrain versions (`src/components/VersionsSection.tsx`, from `data/ensemble`). Then the error budget and the test count; `npm run validation:tests` refreshes the count in `public/validation/tests.json`.
- `/brief`: a printable page for the selected site and period: Explore's answer and its sentences, five numbers (sunlight, contact with Earth, longest night, time with power, battery when full), the Sun's and Earth's rows over the period, the lander, the method and the data, and a paragraph written from the numbers (by a model when one answers and every claim checks out against the figures, otherwise by a template, with the first thing that did not match). The rows keep their black background in print.
- `/learn`: "How it works". The home page's three drawings, the science behind them (the Moon's tilt, libration, how to read the charts, the battery sum) and five steps through the pages (`src/lib/learn/story.ts`), then the way to the site list. It also carries a 150 % projector tip and "Running it offline" (`#offline`).

Every panel ends with a row naming its NASA data (`src/components/Sources.tsx`): LOLA 20 m south of 87.55° S and 80 m elsewhere, JPL DE440, "checked: JPL Horizons" as a link to Accuracy, the NASA CGI Moon Kit for the 3D globe, and on the site list the Artemis III regions and the CLPS mission records.

The 404 and error pages and the plain words for a data failure (`DataProblem`) say what happened and where to go next. The app has an icon and a share image, a title for each page, and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` (see `.env.example`) sets the address the share image links to.
- `/api/chat`: the assistant (task `plan`) and the brief narrator (task `narrate`) on OpenRouter; see `.env.example`. Without a key, or with `?demo=1`, answers come from `src/lib/ai/transcripts.json` and the phrase matcher.

Time with power (`src/lib/engine/landing.ts`) is the hours from landing, with the battery as charged as the lander settings say (full by default), to the first hour the battery cannot cover, counted up to the length of the period. Its Python reference is `pipeline/lunar_pipeline/landing.py`; `uv run python -m lunar_pipeline.cli fixture-landing --site ID [--start UTC] [--days N]` (from `pipeline/`) writes a golden file into `data/fixtures/landing/` that the TypeScript tests check against.

Terrain and shadows. `uv run python -m lunar_pipeline.cli terrain` (from `pipeline/`) cuts a height window for every polar site in the catalogue (20 m per pixel inside 87.55° S, 80 m elsewhere), an 80 m context grid around each 20 m window and the 960 m south cap, listed in `data_out/terrain/manifest.json` (version 3: each 20 m window names its 80 m context); `sync-local` copies them to `public/data/terrain` (git-ignored; tests that need them skip without them). The shadow map is one sweep per grid along the Sun's direction, keeping the running maximum of the tangent height minus distance times the tangent of the Sun's elevation, coarse to fine (`src/lib/engine/shadow.ts`): a ±230 km crop of the cap, the context, then the window, so hills outside the window still shade it. It runs in `src/workers/shadow.worker.ts` through `src/lib/shadow-client.ts` (an overtaken hour's picture is dropped): half resolution at once and full resolution once the hour rests (`useShadowPicture`), measured in the browser at about 37 ms and 135 ms for a 1024 by 1024 window. The 3D terrain is painted with the same picture instead of GPU shadows. Its Python reference is `pipeline/lunar_pipeline/shadow.py`; `uv run python -m lunar_pipeline.cli fixture-shadow` writes `data/fixtures/shadow/`, and `shadow-acceptance.test.ts` checks that de Gerlache Rim 2's pixel is lit when the engine has the Sun up in at least 97 % of the hours of January 2027. `next.config.ts` makes the browser check `/data` files again on every load, since the pipeline rewrites them under the same names.

## The sky (`src/lib/sky/`)

The stand view's maths, each piece tested against SPICE or the engine:

- `frames.ts`: the site's east, north and up in the Moon-fixed frame; azimuth and elevation to world directions (x east, y up, z south).
- `moonOrientation.ts`: the Moon's orientation every 6 hours (`data/sky/moon_me_6h.f32`, J2000 to MOON_ME from SPICE, 2024 to 2036), slerped; 60 off-grid times within 0.001° of SPICE.
- `stars.ts`: the Yale Bright Star Catalogue (`data/sky/stars.bin`, 8,404 stars to magnitude 6.5) placed for a site and hour; within 0.01° of SPICE at two sites and five hours.
- `earth.ts`: Earth's lit fraction and its turn, by the IAU model SPICE uses for IAU_EARTH (within 0.05°).
- `terrainField.ts` and `shadowRule.ts`: the shadow levels as heights in the site's frame (curvature dropped), and the per-pixel shadow rule the ground shader runs: the steepest rise towards the Sun against the Sun's elevation, the Sun's disc for the penumbra, samples from beyond the point's own cell out to 150 km. Its TypeScript twin agrees with the engine on 712 of the 720 hours of January 2027 at de Gerlache Rim 2 (the misses: the Sun half hidden at sunset and sunrise).
- `shaders.ts` and `standRenderer.ts`: the scene (ground meshes from 20 m to 960 m cells out to 125 km, the far skyline as a ring, the stars, the Sun at its true 0.53° with a faint glare, Earth) with regolith light (Lommel–Seeliger, a lunar phase curve, the opposition surge), metre-scale craters and stones drawn near the eye that cast their own shadows, and your own shadow (a person 1.8 m tall). The exposure stops down as a camera's would, looking down-Sun at sunlit ground or with the Sun in the frame; by day the stars show only once the view looks up. The guides: the period's Sun and Earth paths as thin bands, solid where they clear the skyline and faint where the hills hide them, the level horizon all round, and a ring where a hidden Sun or Earth sits (`setGuides`). `buildScene` needs no WebGL, so the camera and the labels are tested in Node. Part D2 (immersive design, section 17): the measured ground's meshes live in `groundLevels.ts`, each reading its own three height slots (its level, the next, the cap last) and cut round, its light fading into the next level over the ring inside its edge, and its shadows too, so no square shows. The lander (`landerMesh.ts`, its shadow shape in `landerShape.ts`) has braced legs on dish feet, tanks, thruster pods, payloads and creased gold and silver foil whose glints are capped under the bloom, and its shaded sides are filled by the sunlit dust by day. The rocks (`boulders.ts`) are stones cut by planes in random directions, rounded and bedded, in three kinds. At night with neither the Sun nor Earth up the ground is lit by the sky it sees (slopes by how much they face up, crater bowls darker than their rims) and the rocks and the lander from above, with a faint key light from 50° up and 90° round from the Sun so the stones keep their facets (`NIGHT` in `shaders.ts`). Part D3 (section 18): the opening turns up to 30° towards the skyline's highest point with the Sun and the lander kept in frame (`standOpening.ts`); the moving frames are drawn at a pixel ratio paced by the frames' times (`RatioPacer` in `framePacer.ts`: lowered after three slow frames in a row, raised again after thirty fast ones); a lander changed in today's card is swapped into the renderer the view has (`setScene`, built beside the scene on the screen and put in its place once its shaders have compiled) rather than drawn by a new one; the shaders are compiled with the composer's target bound, which took 15-28 s off a first visit; `setBright` triples the exposure (`BRIGHT_GAIN`) for projectors.
- `landerShape.ts`: the drawn lander's shape in one table; the model is built from it and the ground shader casts its soft shadow from it (the Sun's disc as the penumbra), so the two always match.
- `boulders.ts`: fourteen drawn boulders and 360 pebbles, seeded by the site and kept clear of the lander and the line to it; the ground shader casts the boulders' shadows.
- `framePacer.ts`: when the view draws: every frame while something moves, one sharp frame after 300 ms of rest, then nothing; a slow graphics chip never queues frames it has not finished. It also says when the view has come to rest (`atRest`), even when the moving frames were already at full sharpness.

`uv run python -m lunar_pipeline.cli fetch --group sky`, then `sky` and `fixture-sky` (from `pipeline/`), write `data/sky/` and `data/fixtures/sky/`; `sync-local` copies `data/sky` to `public/data/sky`.

## The descent (`src/components/home/`)

The home page opens with the descent (spec 23.1): a section six screens tall whose picture stays in the window while the page scrolls. One WebGL picture, the Moon, turns 1¼ times, tips its south pole towards the camera and comes closer on a log scale, from the whole Moon to the 500 km relief map of the pole (its places named) to de Gerlache Rim 2's 20 km shadow picture, all three lying on the one globe in the pipeline's south polar stereographic projection; then the answer for the site. One line of words stands at each stage, never two at once, and a rail at the side names the stages and jumps to any of them. "Fly to the south pole" plays it in 11 seconds (a wheel, a touch, a key or the page scrolled by any other hand stops it; at the end the answer takes the focus). With reduced motion the picture shows each stage's own view with nothing gliding or turning, and "Fly to the south pole" lands at once. The words are page text in reading order; without JavaScript "Fly to the south pole" is a link to `/sites`.

- `Descent.tsx`: the section, the words, Play, the rail, reduced motion and the stills. The first screen's words are measured (the text itself) at every size of the window, and the Moon takes the largest room they leave, right of them on a wide screen or under them on a tall one, never under the title, the sentence or the buttons; Earth stands beside it, clear of the words, the rail and Luna (`firstMoon`, `earthSpot` in `src/lib/viz/descentPose.ts`).
- `DescentScene.tsx`: the live picture (three.js, through `next/dynamic` without server rendering). It glides after the scroll (a 0.14 s time constant) so a wheel's steps never jolt it, and every move eases in and out; nothing moves before the first screen's words have gone.
- `src/lib/viz/descentPose.ts`: the stages, the pose at any progress and the projection, with no data file; `src/lib/viz/descent.ts`: the words, worked out on the server.
- `public/hero/moon-still.jpg` and `earth-still.jpg`: the Moon and Earth cut from a still of the scene, placed where the words leave room: the first paint, and what stays without WebGL (with the relief map and the site's picture further down, and a line on the first screen that says so). The textures and their credits are in `public/hero/textures/`.

The arrival it replaced (`Arrival.tsx`, `ArrivalCanvas.tsx`, its stills and its `?arrival=` capture) went on 14 November, and the rest of it went later that day: the globe it drew (`src/lib/sky/globe.ts`, `src/lib/data/globe.ts`, the pipeline's `globe` command and its download), its flight path (`src/lib/sky/arrivalPath.ts`), the view from the site's flight mode (`setPose` and the views from above in `standRenderer.ts`) and the 240 m flight level the terrain command cut for it.

## Themes

Dark is the default (part C of the immersive design); the header switch changes it and the choice is kept in `localStorage` under `earthrise.theme`, so a visitor who chose light keeps it. The light theme stays for print and classrooms, and the printable brief is always light. `?theme=dark` or `?theme=light` sets the theme for one visit without changing the stored choice. The system preference is not followed. An inline script in `src/app/layout.tsx` sets `data-theme` on `<html>` before the first paint (`src/lib/theme.ts`). Colours are tokens in `src/app/globals.css` (the dark values also apply inside `.theme-dark`, which the stand view uses so its time bar stays dark under the black sky); picture panels ("stages": charts on black, the shadow map, 3D) keep the same dark colours in both themes. The design is in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-01-ui-redesign-design.md`.

Text never scales with a picture: labels on charts and drawings are page text laid over the drawing (`src/components/SkyRow.tsx`), so nothing falls under 13 px on a narrow screen.

The look, in short (reasoning in section 15 of the redesign, and sections 6 and 15 of the immersive design): the palette is the Moon's own three tones, shadow black for picture panels, regolith grey for the light page and sunlit white for its panels, with colour kept for the Sun (gold) and Earth (blue). In the dark theme the page is near-black (`#07080a`), the stages true black, and panels are dark plates one step above the page with a hairline edge; body text is 7:1 on them and secondary text 4.5:1 in both themes (`src/app/globals.test.ts`). Spec 23's Moonstone look (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-14-moonstone-redesign.md`) puts black and white with one moonstone blue over it: glows are the accent's own light, nothing is blurred behind a plate, and every animation is defined in `globals.css` and stopped under reduced motion (`src/app/look.test.ts`). Faint real stars from the stand view's catalogue run behind the header and each page's title (`StarBand`, `src/lib/sky/starBand.ts`): the low sky over the south pole, drawn on a canvas down to the element marked `data-band-end`, in the dark theme only. Headlines use the class `display` (Instrument Serif with its italic), the odd word `pixel` (Pixelify Sans), reading text IBM Plex Sans and labels `eyebrow` (IBM Plex Mono); the four are self-hosted in `src/app/fonts`, so a build needs no network. Pictures have a 6 px radius, panels and controls 8 px. The descent moves only as the page scrolls or after "Fly to the south pole" is pressed; reduced motion shows still frames.

## Assistant and narration

The assistant is the panel "Ask in plain words" on Explore and Compare (`src/components/PlannerChat.tsx`). `src/lib/ai/`: `actions.ts` (closed action schema, validator, site matcher, `applyActions`, the notes in the pages' words), `intents.ts` (twelve rehearsed phrasings, among them "make it 90 days" and "find the best landing date for 14 days"), `explanations.ts` (five fixed paragraphs), `state-summary.ts` (the compact state sent with each question), `openrouter.ts` (model chain with timeouts), `protocol.ts` (system prompt, lenient JSON extraction, reply types), `cache.ts` + `transcripts.json` (twelve rehearsed answers), `rate-limit.ts`, `narrate.ts` (template brief, prompts), `claims.ts` (the claim check), `client.ts` (browser calls). The claim check drops any sentence of a reply whose claims the app's figures do not hold: a number on the wrong measure, unit, site or sign, a wrong state or comparison, a cause the figures do not give, or a survival claim. The example questions under the box ask at once when clicked and come back when nothing answered. While a question is out, a wait row shows it, the seconds, and after 8 s "Free models can be slow; if none answers, the app replies from its own phrasings." When a key is set and every model fails, the reply is the busy one (`reason: "model-busy"`). "Or ask in plain words" on the answer jumps to the box (`#ask`). `AI_MODEL_TIMEOUT_MS` sets the time one model gets and `AI_MODEL_BUDGET_MS` the total per request (see `.env.example`). The catalogue the server validates against is `src/lib/data/catalogue.json`, written by the pipeline's `sync-local`.

## Power and contact

- `src/lib/engine/power.ts`: irradiance, panel geometries, battery simulation (`powerBudget`), pinned to `data/fixtures/power/` written by `pipeline/lunar_pipeline/power.py`.
- `src/lib/engine/metrics.ts`: `withCommsThreshold`, `contactWindows`, `windowScore`, and `Series.sunAu`.
