# Method (Week 1 reference implementation)

## Sun and Earth positions
- SPICE DE440 (`de440s.bsp`), lunar orientation `moon_pa_de440_200625.bpc`, frame `MOON_ME` (alias of `MOON_ME_DE440_ME421`), aberration `LT+S`, observer = Moon centre.
- Hourly table 2024-01-01T00:00 → 2036-12-31T23:00 (113,976 steps), float32 km, Catmull-Rom interpolation between samples (error below 0.002° at half steps).
- Topocentric az/el from a site on the 1737.4 km sphere with the site offset subtracted (Earth parallax up to 0.26°). Sun/Earth directions are evaluated with site height 0 m (matches the Horizons fixtures at alt = 0); the DEM height + mast enter the horizon mask only. Effect on az/el is below 0.001°.

## Terrain horizon
- LOLA polar DEMs (20 m to 6 km, 80 m to 100 km, 240 m to 220 km), 720 azimuths, great-circle march, exact spherical curvature, bilinear sampling. Observer mast 2 m for the featured skylines; 0 to 10 m in the browser.
- Where a ring leaves its DEM (sites near a grid edge, e.g. IM-1 at 80.13°S), the missing distances are re-marched on the next coarser DEM instead of being dropped.
- Sunlit fraction = fraction of the solar disk (radius 0.267°) above the skyline; Earth visible when the Earth centre is above the skyline.

## Terrain completeness and mast height

A skyline is kept, and worked out, by everything that changes it: the site's position, the mast height and the terrain source. A featured skyline (`data/masks`, made by the pipeline at 2 m) stands for 2 m only. At any other height the browser worker works out the skyline of every site a shown result uses: the selected site, the compared sites, the site list (and with it Explore's nearby search) and the year search. Going from 2 m to 5 m lowers the featured skylines by 0.01° to 0.64° on average and by up to 2.2° in one direction (the peak near Cabeus B), so a 2 m skyline is not a 5 m one. The worker runs one wanted job at a time (the selected site first, then the compared sites, then the list); a job for a height or a site nobody shows any more is dropped before its turn, and one already running is let finish without holding up the rest. Every change of settings commits the settings, the results and the sites still waiting in one update, so no state shows a result from older settings; while a site waits, the page says so ("Working out the hills seen from a 5 m mast for 18 sites… The figures below are still from 2 m.") and every result carries the height its skyline is seen from. A skyline that cannot be had says why: the tiles are not on this server, a tile failed to load, or the point lies north of 60° S.

Every skyline also carries a terrain report to every result (the verdict, each compared site, each row of the site list, the brief):

- Provenance: a featured file (its name, when the pipeline made it, at its mast height), a skyline worked out in the browser from the tiles (the mast height, when the tile set was cut), or a flat horizon assumed for a site outside the polar grids (the same from any mast).
- Coverage: each of the 720 rays is counted in 120 m steps out to 220 km (the 240 m grid's half pixel); a step has terrain when any band, primary or fallback, gave a height in it. The report gives the share of steps with terrain, the missing share within 6 km, from 6 to 100 km and from 100 to 220 km, and the nearest missing step.
- Open rays: a missing step counts as no hill, so it can only lower the skyline. It matters only where a hill there could stand above the skyline found; the bound is the highest ground on the Moon (10,786 m above the 1737.4 km sphere) placed at that step. A ray with such a step is open: its skyline is not established.
- Fallback: the bands that took distances from a coarser grid, and the grid the site's own height came from.
- Tiles: per skyline, the tiles the march needed that the server does not have (HTTP 404, a missing file: absent) and those whose load failed (any other error: failed). Every tile of the three grids exists in the manifest, so an absent tile is always a loss.

Each result is then complete (every step has terrain from the grid planned for its distance, no tile absent or failed), degraded (the skyline is established everywhere, but a coarser grid stood in for part of a ray or for the site height, a tile was filled from a coarser grid, steps are missing only where no hill could rise above the skyline, or a flat horizon is assumed) or incomplete (at least one open ray, or a computed skyline without the march's record). The threshold for incomplete is one open ray rather than a share: a missing tile is 41 km (80 m grid) to 123 km (240 m grid) wide and opens rays by the dozen, never one, and one hill in the Sun's path shortens the sunlight by hours. Because missing terrain can only overstate the hours of sunlight and of Earth in view, an incomplete result names no better landing day, no better place nearby, no best site for a measure on Compare (a site with complete terrain that reaches the same top is still named), no longest time with power on the site list and no best date in the next 12 months, and says why; on the site list it is ranked after the others in its group. Degraded results and flat horizons are shown with their limit in words and may still be recommended.

Measured: worked out again from the tiles, all 18 featured skylines have terrain at every one of their 1,319,760 steps and no open ray; only IM-1's takes part of its skyline from the 240 m grid, from 3.9 to 100 km (the 80 m grid ends 4 km north of it). Before, the march counted every sample of a band that ran off its grid as missing even where a fallback band covered it, and called IM-1 32 % missing (1,573,616 of 4,895,280 samples). Dropping one tile under the Shackleton rim summit: the 80 m tile is filled from the 240 m grid from 6 km out (nothing missing, degraded); the 240 m tile leaves 95 steps without terrain, all 100 to 220 km out, and opens 7 rays (incomplete). Under de Gerlache Rim 2 a lost 240 m tile leaves 7,598 missing steps but opens no ray (degraded); under Malapert Massif it leaves 19 and opens 3 (incomplete). A skyline takes 0.2 to 1.0 s to work out in Node from local tiles, so a new mast height on the site list (18 polar sites) takes some seconds in a browser.

## Cross-checks (from `data/fixtures/horizons/summary.json` and the report command)
- JPL Horizons, 6 sites × 2 bodies × 25 epochs (300 rows, 2025–2026): max |Δel| = 2.7e-5°, max |Δaz·cos el| = 2.6e-6° (about 0.1 arcsec).
- Mazarico et al. 2011 maps at the Shackleton rim high point (−89.781°, −156.257°). Two of our quantities are comparable: `illum_fraction` = fraction of hours with any part of the solar disk above the skyline (0.907 over 2025–2026), and `mean_sun_fraction` = time-averaged visible disk fraction (0.862). The map value `AVGVISIB` = 0.796 (2009–2013 span, 60 m DEM, no mast). Earth visibility: ours 0.500 vs map 0.533. Systematic differences: our point is the 20 m argmax peak (map pixel is 60 m), we add a 2 m mast, and the spans differ; the sign (ours higher) is consistent with all three.
- Shackleton crater centre: 0 % sunlit and 0 % Earth-visible in both our series and the Mazarico maps (permanently shadowed).
- Longest continuous darkness at the Shackleton rim high point over 2025–2026: 63 h; longest Earth gap: 334 h.

## Power budget (Week 4)

Irradiance on a Sun-facing surface is 1361 W/m² · (1 AU / d)² · f, where d is the Sun distance from the DE440 table and f the visible fraction of the solar disk above the terrain skyline (section "Terrain horizon"). Panel output is P = η · A · irradiance · max(0, n · s) with the panel normal n for four geometries: horizontal (n = up, n · s = sin el), vertical fixed at azimuth φ (cos el · cos(az − φ)), vertical turning to the Sun's azimuth (cos el) and two-axis tracking (1). The battery walk uses one-hour steps: E(t+1) = min(C, E(t) + (P − L) · 1 h); an hour whose deficit exceeds the stored energy is a brownout and leaves the battery empty. Reported: minimum state of charge, brownout hours, first brownout hour, and the time with power (for example "power for 3 days 4 hours, then night"). Reference implementation: `pipeline/lunar_pipeline/power.py`; golden fixtures under `data/fixtures/power/` pin `web/src/lib/engine/power.ts` to 1e-6 W. The default configuration (vertical panel turning to the Sun, 4 m², a 30 % panel, 200 W load, 2000 Wh battery) is illustrative of a small lander, not any specific vehicle.

## Direct-to-Earth contact (Week 4)

Earth is in view when its elevation exceeds the skyline at its azimuth by the minimum elevation the planner sets (0–10°). Contact windows are maximal runs of visible hours. Mast height changes the skyline itself: featured skylines are computed with a 2 m mast and stand for that height only; at any other height the browser worker works out the skyline of every site a result shows (see "Terrain completeness and mast height").

## Best window (Week 4)

The finder scans the 365 days after the chosen start date (clamped to the ephemeris table) in 6-hour steps and scores every window of the chosen length as sunlit fraction + Earth-visible fraction − (longest night + longest Earth gap) / (2 × window hours). Only sunlit start hours are candidates, because a lander arrives in daylight; a site that never sees the Sun in the scan falls back to every start. Without this rule a window that begins mid-night wins by truncating the night at its edge.

## Validation (Week 5)

`uv run python -m lunar_pipeline.cli validate` writes three tables into `data/validation/` (synced to the web app and shown on `/validate`):

- `horizons.json`: every stored JPL Horizons row (six sites, Sun and Earth, 2025-2026 every 30 days, `CENTER=coord@301`) next to our own azimuth and elevation; worst difference 2.7e-5° in elevation and 2.6e-6° in azimuth over 300 rows. The page can re-query JPL live for eight epochs in 2026 through `/api/horizons` (a server-side proxy with a 12 s timeout) and falls back to the stored table when JPL is unreachable.
- `mazarico.json`: our sunlit fraction (hourly 2024-2036, 2 m mast) against the LOLA average-visibility map `avgvisib_85s_060m_201608` (60 m/px, south of 85°S) at the 15 covered sites; within 5 points at 11 of them, worst +10.8 points at the Shackleton rim high point. High points read a few points brighter here because the map sees no mast and averages other years.
- `backcasts.json`: the predicted local sunset after each CLPS landing (LOLA skyline, or a flat horizon for the mare site) against the reported end of the mission: Blue Ghost Mission 1 lasted 2.4 h past our predicted sunset (Firefly reported about 5 h), IM-2 died 299 h before its sunset on its side in a crater, IM-1 lost power about three days before its sunset with tilted panels.

Error budget: Sun and Earth directions under 0.01° (measured 3e-5°); terrain skyline 0.05-0.15° (LOLA posts of 20-240 m, 1-2 m height noise, reproduced by the browser engine to 0.01°); event timing ±15-60 minutes near the pole (hourly series, the Sun moves 0.5° per hour along the horizon).
