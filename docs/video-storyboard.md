# 30-second video storyboard — Earthrise

Global judges see the project page, the demo link and this video. No voice-over: captions only, so it reads with the sound off. Record the app at 1360×900 in a clean browser window (kiosk mode, no bookmarks bar), 1080p output, 30 fps, screen capture with OBS or the OS recorder; add captions and cuts in a simple editor. The first 13 seconds are the home page's arrival, the first 13 of the 15 seconds that "Fly to the south pole" takes to fly down to landing; they are captured frame by frame through its capture hook rather than screen-recorded (see the notes). Two iterations are planned: v1 by 8 November, v2 after the rehearsals.

## Notes for the recording

- Record the assistant shot live: a build without `NEXT_PUBLIC_AI_MODE=cached`, `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` in `web/.env.local`, in a fresh tab or with `?demo=0` (demo mode is kept per tab), so the reply's line reads "answered by …". Retake if it reads "matched your words" or "rehearsed answer", and cut the wait row.
- Shots 1 to 3 are the arrival, captured frame by frame so every frame is sharp. On the demo laptop (discrete graphics), serve the production build (`npm run build`, then `npm start`) and open `/?arrival=0&bare=1` in a 1920×1080 view: `?arrival=` holds the flight at a progress from 0 to 5, and `&bare=1` leaves the page's words out (the captions carry them). Let the page finish loading so the measured ground is in, scroll the arrival to the top of the window (the scroll no longer moves the flight) and wait until `window.__arrival` exists. For each of the 390 frames (the first 13 s of the 15 s flight, at 30 fps), set the progress Play reaches at that time with `window.__arrival.setProgress(p)` (`playProgress` in `web/src/lib/sky/arrivalTiming.ts`: 3, 3.5, 2, 3 and 3.5 seconds from stop to stop), await `window.__arrival.sharp()`, which resolves once that frame is drawn at full sharpness, then screenshot the stage. Join the frames at 30 fps.
- For shot 4, on the demo laptop with the production build running, open `/explore/stand?site=de_gerlache_rim_2&start=2027-01-01&days=30&hour=64&bright=1`, let the ground finish building and record about 2 s of the Sun just over the hills with its label ("Sun 1.7° up"). Then open the same address with `hour=217`, press Next ("Next: Earth rises, 10 Jan") and record about 2 s of Earth on the horizon with its caption line. `bright=1` turns on "Brighter picture" (three times the exposure, a stop and a half, also under "More" in today's card), so the night reads on video. The hours come from this month's key moments at de Gerlache Rim 2: the Sun sets behind the hills at hour 67 (3 January, 19:00 UTC) and Earth comes into view at hour 219 (10 January, 03:00 UTC), so hour 64 is three hours before the first and hour 217 two hours before the second. The line "Press Play to watch the month go by · drag to look around" stays over the view until the first drag, Play or Next: nudge the view by a pixel before the hour-64 take, or keep the line in shot. `bright=1` also keeps the Easter egg out of the shot. Confirm that Next reads "Next: Sunset, 3 Jan" at hour 64 and "Next: Earth rises, 10 Jan" at hour 217, and move the hours if the data has changed.
- For shot 5, open Explore with "Open the full forecast" on the arrival's answer (or the home page's de Gerlache Rim 2 place), so the period is 30 days from 1 January 2027 and the lander is the small default one, and add de Gerlache rim summit and de Gerlache Rim 2 to Compare beforehand (the ready pair "A ridge and the summit 12 km away" does it in one click).
- Keep the mouse out of frame between actions; move it only for the click that the caption describes.
- Captions: white on a dark band, one line, 44 px; hold each at least two seconds.
- Export at 1920×1080, H.264, under 50 MB; check it plays on a phone with the sound off.
